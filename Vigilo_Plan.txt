Vigilo
The Self-Healing Humanitarian Logistics Network
Tagline: When plans break, aid keeps moving.

Project type: AI-assisted humanitarian logistics and emergency-response platform
Primary objective: Prevent avoidable aid waste through resilience-aware dispatch, safety enforcement, and automated operational recovery.

1. The Problem

Humanitarian aid distribution is a dynamic logistics problem, not simply a donation-matching problem.

Donors, riders, and receiving centers operate with incomplete information. Donations expire, riders become unavailable, shelters reach capacity, routes become inaccessible, and some supplies cannot be used without specific prerequisites.

Traditional workflows often depend on coordinators manually resolving these changes.

Vigilo is designed to make dispatch decisions continuously, enforce safety constraints, detect operational failures, and recover feasible deliveries without double-allocating supplies.

Core challenges
Oversized donations exceed individual rider capacity or recipient demand.
Unresponsive volunteers leave inventory blocked by abandoned claims.
Shelter demand changes after a rider has picked up the cargo.
Expiry deadlines make some otherwise attractive assignments unsafe.
Supplies are sent to locations that lack the resources to use them.
Floods, traffic, and connectivity failures disrupt delivery plans.
Coordinators lack visibility into why a delivery failed or what should happen next.
2. Product Vision

Vigilo will operate as a resilience-aware logistics control system connecting donors, volunteer riders, shelters, NGOs, and emergency coordinators.

The platform will maintain a live model of:

Available and committed inventory.
Rider availability, capacity, location, and active assignments.
Shelter demand, receiving capacity, and operational readiness.
Item-specific handling requirements and safety deadlines.
Estimated travel and handoff times.
Active claims, deliveries, incidents, and recovery options.

Every dispatch decision will be evaluated for feasibility before optimization.

The system will optimize efficiency only among plans that satisfy mandatory safety, capacity, compatibility, and inventory constraints.

3. The Signature Innovation: Resilience-Aware Dispatch

Most matching systems evaluate a proposed delivery against the current operational state.

Vigilo will additionally evaluate how that delivery plan behaves under plausible disruptions.

Before committing an assignment, the engine can simulate scenarios such as:

The assigned rider stops responding.
The receiving shelter becomes unavailable.
Travel time increases unexpectedly.
A competing donation fulfills the recipient's demand.
A backup destination reaches capacity.
A dependency required to use the supply becomes unavailable.

The engine will compare feasible plans using:

Expected delivery time.
Expected spoilage and unmet demand.
Recovery time after a disruption.
Availability of alternative riders and destinations.
Additional travel caused by contingency plans.
Fairness across eligible receiving centers.
Key principle

A delivery plan is better when it is both efficient under normal conditions and recoverable when conditions change.

Backup options must be conditional rather than duplicate reservations. Inventory and recipient capacity must never be promised simultaneously to conflicting assignments.

This resilience-aware approach is the proposed research contribution. Its novelty and performance must be evaluated against existing implementations and measurable baselines.

5. Core Dispatch Rules

The dispatch engine will enforce hard constraints before optimizing route quality.

Rule 1: Quantity conservation

For each inventory batch:

Available quantity + reserved quantity + picked-up quantity + delivered quantity + other explicitly accounted quantities must reconcile with the batch's original quantity.

Inventory transitions must be transactional.

Rule 2: Rider capacity

The total active load assigned to a rider must not exceed the rider's verified capacity.

Rule 3: Recipient capacity

Incoming allocations must not exceed confirmed remaining demand and receiving capacity.

Rule 4: Safety deadline

For a candidate delivery:

pickup time + travel time + handoff time + safety buffer <= remaining safe-handling time

The applicable safe-handling deadline must be established from item-specific rules and reliable source information.

Rule 5: Compatibility

An assignment must satisfy item eligibility, handling, storage, and recipient prerequisites.

Rule 6: Reservation exclusivity

The same inventory quantity cannot be committed to conflicting active claims.

Rule 7: Recovery feasibility

An in-transit reroute must account for the rider's current position, remaining cargo, alternative demand, recipient capacity, travel time, and safety deadline.

Rule 8: Safe failure

If no feasible assignment exists, stop automatic dispatch, preserve accurate inventory state, and escalate the incident.

The engine must never invent a safe destination merely to complete a workflow.

6. Last-Safe-Decision Clock

Vigilo will introduce a deadline-aware recovery mechanism that estimates when a recovery option will cease to be feasible.

For every candidate destination, the engine evaluates:

Remaining safe-handling time.
Estimated travel and handoff duration.
Safety buffer.
Data freshness and uncertainty.
Time required to confirm the destination.
Time required to assign and notify a replacement rider.

The result is an estimated latest useful decision time.

For example, a batch may have 20 minutes of safe-handling time remaining, while a proposed alternative requires 14 minutes for travel and handoff plus a 3-minute safety buffer.

That leaves only 3 minutes for the remaining recovery work under those assumptions.

The system should escalate before the recovery window closes rather than wait for the original plan to fail completely.

The estimate will be recalculated whenever relevant conditions change.

7. Resource Dependency Graph

Supplies will be represented as items with eligibility and prerequisite rules.

Examples:

Raw ration kits may require verified clean water and cooking fuel.
Temperature-sensitive supplies may require suitable storage and handling.
Certain medical supplies may require a recipient with the necessary authorized handling capability.
A shelter may accept a supply only during staffed receiving hours.

The engine will evaluate whether prerequisites are already available or can be fulfilled before the supply arrives.

If prerequisites cannot be satisfied in time, the engine will find another eligible destination or block dispatch.

The dependency graph will use configurable rules and explicit evidence. AI may assist with extracting information from documents, but it will not override safety rules.

11. State Machines
Unpicked allocation

AVAILABLE → CLAIMED → ACKNOWLEDGED → PICKED_UP

An expired claim may return to AVAILABLE only if the system verifies that the allocation has not been picked up or otherwise committed.

Physical delivery

PICKED_UP → IN_TRANSIT → DELIVERED

A disruption may transition the delivery to:

IN_TRANSIT → REROUTE_PENDING → IN_TRANSIT

If no safe destination exists:

IN_TRANSIT → INCIDENT_REVIEW

The system must preserve physical custody and inventory reconciliation during these transitions.

Important distinction

An unpicked reservation can release inventory back to the dispatch pool.

An in-transit delivery cannot. It requires a destination-change decision, a controlled update, and an auditable record.

12. Chaos Lab: Judge Demonstration

The simulator will support the following scenarios.

Scenario A: Oversized donation
Create a batch of 180 meals.
Provide riders with varying carrying capacities.
Set multiple shelter demands.
Verify that quantities are split without exceeding capacity or demand.
Scenario B: Ghost reservation
Rider claims a batch.
Rider becomes unresponsive.
Simulated time advances.
The lease expires.
The recovery worker releases only eligible unpicked inventory.
A replacement assignment is attempted.
Scenario C: Shelter closure during transit
Rider picks up 40 meals.
Shelter demand drops to zero.
Engine invalidates the destination.
Alternative destinations are checked.
A safe reroute is committed or an incident is escalated.
Scenario D: Expiry infeasibility
Create a batch with a short safe-handling window.
Configure a travel estimate that exceeds the available time.
Verify that dispatch is blocked.
Scenario E: Missing prerequisites
Offer raw ration kits to a shelter without verified water or cooking fuel.
Verify that the assignment is rejected unless the prerequisites can be safely fulfilled.
Scenario F: Cascading failure
A rider becomes unavailable.
An alternative shelter simultaneously closes.
Multiple allocations compete for limited remaining capacity.
The engine recomputes the feasible plan and reconciles all affected inventory.
Scenario G: Flood disruption
Mark a route inaccessible.
Recalculate travel estimates.
Compare alternative routes and destinations.
Verify that infeasible deliveries are blocked.
Scenario H: Concurrency stress
Multiple riders attempt to claim overlapping inventory.
Run concurrent allocation and recovery requests.
Verify that quantities cannot be double-allocated.
13. Evaluation Metrics

The prototype will compare Vigilo against a baseline that uses nearest-feasible-recipient matching without resilience-aware planning.

Metrics will include:

Safe delivery completion rate.
Meals and supplies spoiled.
Unmet recipient demand.
Time to recover after disruption.
Number of duplicate allocation violations.
Number of unsafe assignments accepted.
Additional travel caused by recovery.
Recovery success under varying failure rates.
Fairness of service coverage.
Time required to make a feasible dispatch decision.

Safety and quantity-conservation rules will be tested as invariants, not treated as average performance targets.

No performance improvement will be claimed until the tests produce reproducible results.

Vigilo is ready for judging when:

An oversized batch is allocated without violating capacity or demand.
Unsafe and incompatible assignments are rejected.
Abandoned unpicked claims are recovered safely.
Picked-up inventory is never mistakenly returned to available stock.
Shelter closures trigger a controlled recovery workflow.

17. Final Product Positioning

Vigilo is not another donation marketplace.

It is a resilience-aware coordination and recovery engine for humanitarian logistics.

Its core contribution is the combination of constraint-based dispatch, recoverability-aware planning, deadline-aware recovery decisions, transactional inventory control, and verifiable operational evidence.

The user interface makes the system understandable. The dispatch engine makes it useful. The simulator proves whether it works.

Final tagline: When plans break, aid keeps moving.
